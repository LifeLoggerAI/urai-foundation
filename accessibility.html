Accessibility and inclusion

Human-centered technology should include more people, not fewer.

URAI Foundation treats accessibility as a core design and governance responsibility, not a final checklist item.

Accessibility-first thinking

AI systems should account for visual, auditory, cognitive, mobility, language, literacy, neurodivergent, and sensory accessibility from the beginning of the design process.

Plain-language communication

Important information should be understandable by non-specialists whenever possible, including policies, consent flows, privacy disclosures, and safety limitations.

Inclusive participation

Communities affected by AI systems should have opportunities to review, critique, and improve standards before deployment decisions are finalized.

Continuous improvement

Accessibility work should be ongoing. The Foundation should welcome feedback regarding barriers, readability, compatibility, and usability issues.

Website accessibility

Simple, readable, and navigable by default.

The public site aims to support semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, readable contrast, responsive layouts, reduced complexity, and screen-reader compatibility where practical.

As the website evolves, accessibility testing and review should remain part of the launch and update process.

Accessibility feedback

Help improve accessibility.

If you encounter an accessibility barrier, readability issue, or usability concern, contact the Foundation so it can be reviewed and improved.
