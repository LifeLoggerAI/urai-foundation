Research and initiatives

Research that makes AI easier to question, understand, and govern.

The Foundation focuses on applied public-interest research: practical standards, readable explainers, community review methods, and safeguards for sensitive human contexts.

Consent and data dignity

Research on consent language, opt-in patterns, data minimization, revocation, and user control for systems that handle sensitive life data.

Explainability for daily life

Plain-language methods for helping people understand why an AI system produced an insight, recommendation, classification, or warning.

Accessibility-first AI

Guidance for visual, auditory, cognitive, neurodivergent, mobility, literacy, and language accessibility across AI-supported experiences.

Mental-health safety boundaries

Clear standards for wellness language, crisis redirection, non-diagnostic positioning, and safe escalation when AI systems discuss emotional or mental-health topics.

Community review

Practical models for involving affected communities, nonprofits, researchers, and domain experts before high-impact systems are launched.

Public accountability records

Reusable templates for risk reviews, decision records, incident summaries, transparency updates, and standards versioning.

Publication stance

Open where possible, careful where necessary.

The Foundation should publish standards, summaries, and lessons in accessible language. It should not publish private data, active abuse paths, sensitive security details, or claims that have not been reviewed.

Research outputs should distinguish evidence, hypothesis, policy preference, and product roadmap so readers can understand what is proven and what is still developing.
