Governance & transparency

Important standards should leave a reviewable trail.

The Foundation publishes a formation-stage draft structure for consequential standards work: how decisions may be recorded, how risks may be reviewed, what evidence may support a claim, and how corrections may be requested.

Authority boundary: These public governance materials are formation-stage drafts. Governance roles, governing bodies, and approval processes are not yet constituted. They do not create separate corporate, charitable, fiduciary, regulatory, or tax-exempt authority unless separately verified in governing records and the public status disclosure.

Review principle

A commitment is stronger when a person can see who made it, what evidence supported it, what risks were considered, and how it can be challenged.

Public framework

Six parts of accountable standards work

Decision records

Consequential choices should identify scope, rationale, evidence, unresolved questions, and responsible reviewers.

Risk review

Higher-risk work should document foreseeable harms, mitigations, evidence thresholds, monitoring, and reassessment triggers.

Transparency

Public claims should distinguish source evidence, demonstrated behavior, current limits, and future intent.

Corrections

Material errors should have a visible path for challenge, review, correction, and an updated public record where appropriate.

Accessibility

Review processes should account for sensory, cognitive, communication, and interaction barriers without claiming certification that has not occurred.

Human authority

Automation can support review, but it does not replace approvals that require legal, institutional, community, or accountable human authority.
