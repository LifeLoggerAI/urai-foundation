## 1. Multidisciplinary Advisory Council Charter — Template

**Council name:**
**Charter ID:**
**Version:**
**Status:** Draft / Adopted / Superseded / Retired

### Purpose

The Council exists to provide independent subject-matter review, challenge, recommendations, and documented dissent on matters within its stated scope. It does not replace the Foundation's governing body, product operator, licensed professional relationship, regulator, IRB, or other legally authorized decision-maker.

### Scope

- standards and policy review;
- program and pilot review;
- claims and evidence review;
- accessibility and affected-community review;
- research/scientific review within expertise;
- risk, incident, and exception review when assigned.

### Membership

Members are appointed through a documented process that verifies identity, credentials, role scope, conflicts, term, compensation, confidentiality/data access, and public-listing permission.

### Independence

Members may disagree, recuse, record minority opinions, and decline endorsement. Funding or commercial relationships do not purchase a favorable recommendation.

### Meetings and records

Each material review should preserve agenda, attendees, conflicts/recusals, evidence reviewed, recommendations, disposition, owner, deadline, minority views, and public-safe summary where appropriate.

### Authority

The Council is advisory unless a separately adopted governing instrument delegates a specific decision right. Advisory recommendations become binding only when the authorized decision-maker adopts them under the applicable process.

### Confidentiality

Sensitive personal, research, security, legal, and proprietary data are shared only on a least-necessary basis under applicable controls.

## 6. Confidentiality & Data-Access Addendum

### Access principle

Advisor access is purpose-specific, minimum necessary, time-bounded, logged where technically feasible, and revocable.

| Data/system | Need | Access level | Start/end | Owner approval |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### Prohibited by default

- bulk access to raw user memories or communications;
- credentials, secrets, payment data, government IDs, or unrelated private records;
- use of Foundation access for personal research, training data, publication, or commercial purpose without separate authorization;
- copying sensitive records into personal drives or unmanaged devices.

On role termination or access expiration, access is revoked and controlled copies are returned/deleted according to policy.

## 11. Psychology & Emotional-Safety Review Checklist

For memory, narrator, companion, replay, relationship, grief, identity, legacy, youth, or emotionally intense experiences:

- [ ] Observation, inference, and generation are visibly distinct.
- [ ] System does not diagnose or assign permanent identity labels.
- [ ] System does not present speculation about another person's inner state as fact.
- [ ] Sensitive interpretation requires appropriate permission.
- [ ] Preview / skip / mute / exit controls exist.
- [ ] User can correct, dispute, redact, and delete.
- [ ] No guilt, fear, exclusivity, streak pressure, or attachment manipulation is used to retain engagement.
- [ ] Grief/legacy experiences distinguish generated persona/media from the deceased person.
- [ ] High-intensity content has pacing and non-surprise controls.
- [ ] Youth/dependent-user boundaries are separately reviewed.
- [ ] Escalation/referral language matches the actual product role and does not imply crisis care.
- [ ] Known emotional risks and residual uncertainty are documented.

## 12. Education, Teacher & Youth Review Checklist

- [ ] Intended learner/user age and context are clear.
- [ ] Reading level, vocabulary, and instructions are understandable.
- [ ] Important consent/permission concepts are comprehensible, not merely legally present.
- [ ] Cognitive load, pacing, and navigation are appropriate.
- [ ] Alternative modalities exist where appropriate.
- [ ] Learning/reflection is not mislabeled as therapy, diagnosis, or educational assessment.
- [ ] Youth privacy, guardian boundaries, commercial limits, and data minimization are reviewed.
- [ ] Special-education and neurodiversity considerations are included where relevant.
- [ ] Teachers/educators are not asked to make medical determinations.
- [ ] Student/youth participation in review is safe, voluntary, non-extractive, and age-appropriate.
- [ ] Classroom/institution use does not create hidden surveillance of students.

## 13. Accessibility Affected-User Review Protocol

### Before review

- define critical user flows and target access needs;
- provide accessible test materials and accommodation options;
- explain what feedback can influence and what is out of scope;
- compensate sustained expert/lived-experience labor when funding permits.

### Review domains

- screen reader / semantic structure;
- keyboard and alternative input;
- captions, transcripts, visual alternatives;
- contrast, scaling, readable typography;
- reduced motion and non-immersive mode;
- cognitive load, predictability, plain language;
- timeouts and pacing;
- speech/communication alternatives;
- authentication/recovery accessibility;
- complaint, correction, deletion, and exit flows;
- older devices, low bandwidth, and degraded modes where relevant.

## 14. Community Participation & Compensation Policy — Draft Template

Affected-community participation is expertise, not decoration. The Foundation should seek relevant perspectives without implying that one person represents an entire community.

- Participation is voluntary and informed.
- Purpose, expected time, use of feedback, confidentiality, and public attribution are explained.
- Accessibility/accommodations are provided.
- Sustained expert labor should be compensated when funding permits; reimbursement rules should be clear.
- Participation does not require disclosure of unnecessary medical, legal, identity, or trauma details.
- Participants may withdraw subject to clear record-retention boundaries.
- Feedback dispositions are documented so participation is not performative.
- Minority and dissenting views may be preserved.

## 19. Public Statement for the Advisor Program

URAI Foundation is designing a future advisor program in which independently appointed professional and community voices would challenge assumptions, review evidence, identify risk, improve accessibility, strengthen research and education, and protect user dignity. Prospective participation does not mean that a person, employer, university, hospital, government agency, licensing body, or professional organization has certified or endorsed URAI; any future appointment or endorsement requires a separate authorized public record.
