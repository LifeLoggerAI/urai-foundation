# URAI Foundation — Advisors, Professional Councils & Human Governance Architecture

## Purpose

Define the human expertise structure around URAI Foundation: who should advise it, what each discipline contributes, how professional roles are verified, how decisions flow, how advisors remain independent, and how the Foundation prevents names or credentials from being used to imply authority that has not actually been granted.

> **Status rule:** distinguish (1) people and credentials supported by current records, (2) people identified for the Foundation whose exact public title or appointment is still pending verification, and (3) professional seats the Foundation should recruit. A person is not publicly represented as a director, officer, treating clinician, researcher, employee, spokesperson, or formal advisor unless the relevant appointment and permission exist.

## 1. Why URAI needs multidisciplinary human governance

URAI crosses technology, memory, identity, accessibility, education, psychology, neuroscience, family systems, aging, disability, privacy, ethics, spatial computing, artificial intelligence, and long-duration personal records. No single profession can responsibly govern all of those areas.

Engineers should not be asked to make clinical judgments. Physicians should not be asked to define software architecture. Psychologists should not be treated as privacy counsel. Teachers should not be expected to approve medical research. Community members should not be used as symbolic representation without real influence. Legal reviewers should not substitute for lived experience.

**The Foundation's strength should come from disciplined disagreement, not manufactured consensus.**

## 2. Three-layer human governance model

### Layer A — Formal governance

The legally authorized governing body and officers, once constituted and documented, hold fiduciary and organizational authority.

### Layer B — Independent advisory councils

Advisory councils provide expert review, challenge, recommendations, minority opinions, and subject-matter guidance. Advisors do not automatically become directors or officers and do not gain corporate authority merely because their names appear on a website or in a meeting.

### Layer C — Program and working groups

Time-limited working groups address a specific program, standard, pilot, research protocol, accessibility problem, incident, or technical question. A working group can include professionals, affected users, internal operators, and external experts.

## 3. Prospective people and verification status

Potential advisors are tracked only in restricted intake records until identity, credentials, scope, appointment authority, conflicts, compensation, and public-listing permission are verified. This public architecture intentionally does not name prospective people or imply an association before those gates close.

The Foundation should never transform a conversation, draft, production note, or prospective collaboration into a stronger public appointment claim than the record supports.

## 4. Scientific & Clinical Advisory Council

The Scientific & Clinical Advisory Council should review scientific plausibility, research design, human factors, health-adjacent risk, cognitive effects, vulnerable populations, measurement, claims, and translation boundaries. It is not a clinic and does not create a doctor-patient relationship with URAI users.

### Core disciplines

- clinical neuropsychology;
- psychology;
- translational neuroscience;
- neurology;
- geriatrics / gerontology;
- psychiatry / behavioral health;
- rehabilitation science;
- occupational therapy, speech-language, and cognitive-rehabilitation expertise where relevant.

### Review responsibilities

- claims and safety language;
- scientific hypotheses;
- pilot and study protocols;
- measurement frameworks;
- exclusion/escalation boundaries;
- memory-reconstruction risk;
- emotional and cognitive load;
- older-adult, TBI, grief, and vulnerable-user safeguards;
- research publications and evidence interpretation;
- questions requiring qualified external clinical/regulatory review.

### Not the council's default role

- diagnose URAI users;
- provide therapy or medical care;
- prescribe treatment;
- certify the product as medically safe/effective;
- represent FDA, VA, a university, hospital, or licensing board;
- convert exploratory data into clinical claims.

## 5. Psychology, Human Development & Emotional Safety Council

URAI's memory, identity, narrator, relationship, replay, legacy, grief, and companion systems make psychology a distinct governance domain.

Recommended expertise includes clinical psychology, counseling psychology, developmental psychology, social/personality psychology, grief/bereavement, family systems, trauma-sensitive design, behavioral science, HCI, AI anthropomorphism/dependency, and psychometrics.

Primary review questions include whether an experience could intensify shame, grief, fear, fixation, paranoia, dependency, or interpersonal conflict; whether observation is distinguished from interpretation; whether the system avoids permanent identity labels and unsupported claims about another person's inner state; and whether users can preview, interrupt, correct, mute, skip, delete, and leave.

## 6. Education, Teachers & Learning Council

Teachers and educators bring daily expertise in comprehension, explanation, learner differences, cognitive load, and inclusive learning environments.

A future educator-practice voice within the Education, Learning & Human Development Council remains subject to identity, credentials, exact title, public-listing permission, and formal appointment verification.

Recommended seats include classroom teachers, higher-education faculty/instructors, special-education professionals, instructional-design/learning-sciences experts, literacy/plain-language specialists, student-support professionals, developmental/child-learning experts, neurodiversity/learning-accessibility specialists, parent/guardian voices, and safely governed student/young-adult participation where appropriate.

Responsibilities include reviewing educational explanations, onboarding and consent comprehension, youth/future-planning concepts, cognitive load, reading level, multimodal learning, safe classroom/library/community uses, and barriers caused by disability, language, bandwidth, device, or literacy.

## 7. Accessibility & Disability Advisory Council

Accessibility should be governed by people with professional and lived expertise. No single disability representative should be treated as speaking for an entire community.

Relevant perspectives include Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, blind/low-vision users, motor/alternative-input users, cognitive accessibility, neurodivergence, TBI/cognitive fatigue, speech/communication disabilities, older adults and memory change, motion/XR sensitivity, plain-language/low-literacy access, and low-bandwidth/older-device access.

The council should be able to identify barriers, require documented remediation plans before accessibility claims are made, and preserve minority concerns. A checklist alone is not proof of conformance.

## 8. Veteran, Caregiver, Senior & Community Advisory Councils

Community councils provide experience-based review for people directly affected by proposed programs. Participation should be voluntary, accessible, non-extractive, and compensated for sustained expert labor when funding permits.

- **Veteran lane:** veterans, military-family members, transition specialists, disability advocates, and veteran-serving professionals; no implied VA approval or universal representation.
- **Caregiver/family lane:** family caregivers, aging professionals, dementia-care advocates, social workers, and family members; focus on consent/capacity, family conflict, sharing, legacy, and caregiver burden.
- **Senior/older-adult lane:** older adults should participate directly in usability, accessibility, consent, pacing, and value decisions.
- **Youth/family lane:** age-appropriate governance, child-development expertise, privacy safeguards, guardian boundaries, and safely governed youth voice.

## 9. Ethics, Bioethics & Human Rights Council

Review areas include dignity, autonomy, informed consent, memory/identity integrity, relationship and third-party rights, grief/posthumous data, synthetic media, children/dependent users, fairness/cultural context, emotional dependency, surveillance/coercive-use scenarios, proportionality, research ethics, and participant respect.

## 10. Privacy, Security & Data-Rights Council

This council should cover consent/purpose limitation, retention/deletion propagation, export/portability, provider data-use policies, access controls, third-party/bystander data, location/voice/face/biometric/spatial data, coercive-partner and insider threats, incident response, private reporting, and least-privilege advisor/researcher access.

## 11. AI, Engineering & Product-Safety Council

Technical reviewers translate Foundation principles into buildable evidence: exact source/release identity, provider/model versions, data flows, consent enforcement, tests, failure modes, kill switches, rollback paths, observability, and known gaps.

Relevant expertise includes AI/ML evaluation, software/platform architecture, agentic systems, multi-model orchestration, data/cloud engineering, mobile/background processing, AR/VR/XR, synthetic-media provenance, security, and release engineering.

## 12. Research governance and IRB boundary

Scientific advice is not automatically formal human-subject research oversight. An advisory board can identify an inappropriate, underpowered, inaccessible, or ethically weak study, but it is not automatically an Institutional Review Board.

If work constitutes human-subject research, obtain the appropriate independent ethics/research determination and institutional approvals rather than implying that an internal advisory council provides equivalent authority.

A research-governance package should cover aims/hypotheses, protocol/population, inclusion/exclusion, consent, data management, privacy/security, accessibility, risk/benefit, measurement/statistics, incident routes, publication/correction, and independent ethics review where required.

## 13. Legal, nonprofit, IP & regulatory advisory lane

Qualified legal and tax professionals should review entity authority, nonprofit/tax matters, fundraising, contracts, research, employment/contractor issues, IP, privacy, regulated claims, insurance, and governance. Legal reviewers advise on law and authority; they do not substitute for scientific or community judgment.

## 14. Finance, grants & institutional development

Required expertise can include nonprofit finance/accounting, grant strategy/administration, federal research funding, philanthropy, restricted funds, fiscal sponsorship, program budgeting, audit/internal controls, infrastructure credits, and institutional development.

Fundraising staff must not control standards outcomes. Material funding relationships and conflicts should be disclosed.

## 15. Advisor appointment and credential protocol

Before a public advisor profile is activated:

1. verify full professional identity and preferred public name;
2. verify degrees, relevant licenses/certifications, specialty, institution, and public professional source;
3. define exact Foundation title, council/working group, responsibilities, authority, non-responsibilities, and reporting line;
4. disclose employment, ownership, grants, vendors, personal relationships, product interests, and other conflicts;
5. record term, renewal, resignation, and removal process;
6. record compensation status;
7. define confidentiality and least-necessary data access;
8. obtain permission to publish name, credentials, title, biography, headshot, affiliation, and quotes;
9. preserve the advisor's independence and right to disagree/recuse;
10. conduct annual credential/conflict/activity/public-profile review.

## 18. Advisor safeguards

- an advisor's name cannot imply endorsement beyond documented scope;
- credentials cannot be used to imply clinical approval, medical validation, or institutional endorsement without separate evidence;
- advisory service does not create doctor-patient, therapist-client, teacher-student, attorney-client, or research-participant relationships with end users unless separately established;
- advisors do not receive raw private user data merely to be close to the product;
- access is purpose-specific, minimized, authorized, logged where feasible, and revocable;
- advisors can flag unsafe claims, disagree, recuse, and record minority opinions;
- former advisors are removed promptly from current rosters while historical records retain dates/status.

## Public-facing statement

**URAI Foundation is building a multidisciplinary human governance layer around personal AI. Scientists, psychologists, medical professionals, educators, accessibility experts, privacy and security professionals, ethicists, researchers, caregivers, veterans, older adults, families, and other affected communities are not decorative endorsements. Their job is to challenge assumptions, define boundaries, review evidence, improve accessibility, shape research, protect human dignity, and make sure increasingly powerful personal AI remains answerable to people.**

Before any advisor is publicly identified or exercises authority, professional credentials must be verified, scope defined, conflicts disclosed, appointment documented, and publication permission recorded. The future operating process must preserve dissent and must not use any person's name to imply a stronger approval than they actually gave.
