# URAI Foundation

## Master Business Model, Public-Interest Operating Structure, and Partnership Dossier

**Audience:** senior technology, open-source, cloud, infrastructure, research, accessibility, philanthropic, institutional, and strategic-partnership leaders.

**Purpose:** explain why URAI Foundation exists, how it fits into the wider URAI ecosystem, what has already been built, how its public-interest operating model works, how it is intended to be funded, what it can offer partners, and which capabilities remain formation-stage or activation-gated.

> **Evidence boundary:** this document intentionally distinguishes working infrastructure from formation-stage governance and future programs. It does not claim federal tax-exempt status, completed state-status authority, active grant awards, active human-subject research, clinical validation, certification authority, or active public programs where those claims are not yet supported by authoritative evidence.

---

## 1. Executive thesis

URAI Foundation exists to ensure that the most human parts of personal AI — memory, identity, relationships, consent, accessibility, personal agency, emotional safety, life narrative, and long-term digital dignity — are governed by a public-interest layer that is structurally distinct from commercial product development.

URAI is a working technology ecosystem. The Foundation is the public-interest institution being built around that ecosystem: the place where ethical standards are written, versioned, reviewed, challenged, published, and translated into evidence requirements for real products.

The Foundation is not designed as a conventional software company. Its operating model is **public-interest trust infrastructure**. Its core outputs are standards, governance frameworks, risk reviews, accessibility guidance, open documentation, evidence requirements, research and pilot protocols, public-accountability records, community participation mechanisms, education, and eventually carefully governed mission-aligned programs.

The central principle is simple:

> The more intimate and powerful an AI system becomes, the more important it is that dignity, consent, identity, memory, accessibility, safety, transparency, and recourse are not treated as optional product features that can be quietly traded away.

URAI Foundation is the institution intended to hold that line.

## 2. Why the Foundation exists

URAI operates in a category where ordinary software governance is not enough. Personal AI can touch memories, voice, images, relationships, life history, behavior, location, emotional context, identity, goals, family stories, accessibility needs, spatial environments, synthetic media, and long-duration personal records.

That creates five structural problems the Foundation is designed to address.

### Commercial-conflict problem

Commercial product organizations are rewarded for growth, speed, revenue, engagement, and adoption. Those incentives can conflict with restraint, privacy, deletion, accessibility, user autonomy, uncertainty, or the decision not to build a feature. The Foundation creates a separate public-interest surface whose standards and evidence requirements are intended to survive commercial pressure.

### Intimate-data problem

Personal AI is different from generic software because its outputs can affect how people understand themselves and other people. Memory provenance, uncertainty, correction, deletion, third-party rights, and user confirmation therefore become first-order governance issues.

### Vulnerable-user problem

Older adults, disabled users, Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, neurodivergent users, caregivers, veterans, youth, grieving people, and people under cognitive or emotional load can encounter different risks and barriers. Accessibility and safe interaction cannot be treated as a late compliance step.

### AI-truth problem

Generative systems can reconstruct, infer, summarize, or dramatize. In a memory system, a polished reconstruction can feel true even when it is uncertain or generated. The Foundation therefore requires clear separation between recorded fact, imported record, model inference, generated reconstruction, uncertainty, dispute, and user correction.

### Institutional-trust problem

Governments, universities, nonprofits, researchers, accessibility organizations, infrastructure providers, and other serious institutions need more than founder assurances. The Foundation turns principles into versioned standards, review records, evidence states, public corrections, conflict disclosures, implementation packages, and traceable history.

## 3. Mission

URAI Foundation's mission is to advance responsible, accessible, transparent, human-centered AI while protecting dignity, consent, memory, identity, relationships, community, and public trust.

Operationally, the Foundation exists to:

- create and maintain public-interest AI standards;
- protect user autonomy and meaningful choice;
- define data-rights and consent expectations;
- establish memory, identity, relationship, grief, legacy, and synthetic-media safeguards;
- make accessibility a core product and governance requirement;
- create risk-review processes for high-impact AI;
- require transparency, explainability, contestability, correction, deletion, and recourse;
- establish evidence requirements for products claiming alignment;
- support research and carefully bounded pilots without overstating clinical claims;
- create systems for incidents, exceptions, corrections, appeals, and reassessment;
- include affected communities in standards development;
- help the wider URAI ecosystem scale without allowing commercial convenience to outrank human rights.

## 4. What the Foundation is — and is not

### It is

A public-interest standards, ethics, governance, accessibility, research-framing, accountability, and community-benefit institution inside the wider URAI ecosystem.

It is also an institutional bridge: researchers, funders, universities, nonprofits, accessibility experts, technology companies, public agencies, advisors, and communities can engage URAI through a public-interest surface rather than only through a commercial product company.

### It is not

- the commercial operator of the URAI product;
- a medical provider, therapy service, crisis service, diagnostic system, clinical predictor, or medical device;
- currently a certification body, regulator, assurance company, or pay-to-approve standards business;
- a data broker;
- a vehicle for selling user memories, relationship graphs, identity profiles, or sensitive behavioral data;
- a mechanism that lets sponsors buy favorable standards or suppress findings;
- a substitute for qualified legal, tax, research, accessibility, security, or clinical review.

## 5. Position inside the URAI ecosystem

The wider URAI estate is intentionally separated into legible functional layers.

- **URAI Labs LLC** — operating and technology-development company; builds and commercializes products, infrastructure, experiences, and services.
- **URAI IP Holdings** — intended intellectual-property stewardship and licensing layer under separately documented authority.
- **URAI Privacy** — privacy, consent, data-governance, retention, deletion/export, and user-rights enforcement.
- **URAI Foundation** — public-interest ethics, standards, governance, accessibility, research, accountability, and community-benefit layer.
- **URAI Admin** — administrative and operational control surfaces.
- **URAI Analytics** — measurement and analytics subject to consent and privacy rules.
- **URAI Spatial** — true 3D, AR/VR/XR, camera, microphone, location, embodied, environmental, and immersive systems.
- **URAI Studio / Content / Storytime / Asset Factory** — creative, narrative, media-generation, content, and world-building systems.
- **URAI Jobs / agentic systems / Communications / B2B** — autonomous workflows, external operations, organizational interfaces, communications, and enterprise systems.

The Foundation does not need to own or operate each product. Its role is to define what evidence those systems should produce when they claim alignment with public-interest standards.

## 6. Business model: public-interest trust infrastructure

The Foundation creates value by reducing the ethical, accessibility, governance, reputational, research, and institutional risk associated with intimate personal AI.

Its economic engine is not the monetization of user data. Its economic engine is attracting mission-aligned resources that fund public goods, independent review, community participation, accessibility, research, and accountability.

### Primary value created

- open standards;
- governance rules;
- ethical AI principles;
- risk-review methods;
- accessibility frameworks;
- claims and safety boundaries;
- research and pilot protocols;
- public-accountability templates;
- provenance and evidence requirements;
- community-review mechanisms;
- incident, exception, correction, and appeal processes;
- product integration contracts;
- responsible-AI publications and education;
- open-source governance infrastructure;
- future mission-aligned public-interest programs.

### Who receives value

**Users** receive stronger rights, transparency, consent, correction, deletion, export, interruption, and recourse.

**Developers** receive clearer requirements and reusable governance primitives.

**URAI commercial teams** receive an externalized standards/evidence framework that reduces ambiguity and improves institutional credibility.

**Researchers** receive a structured environment for work on memory, identity, accessibility, narrative continuity, spatial AI, and human-AI interaction without collapsing research into product marketing.

**Nonprofits and communities** receive a partner surface organized around public benefit rather than sales.

**Funders** receive a versioned, evidence-oriented institution capable of separating program claims from commercial claims.

**Technology partners** receive a governance counterpart that can translate responsible-AI commitments into implementation requirements.

## 7. Funding and sustainability model

The intended portfolio is deliberately diversified so that no single sponsor controls the Foundation.

Tracked or appropriate channels include:

- federal research and innovation grants;
- aging, caregiver, disability, accessibility, rehabilitation, and veteran-focused funding;
- public-interest AI funding;
- university and research collaborations;
- philanthropic and community-foundation support;
- restricted program funding;
- pilot sponsorship;
- infrastructure and cloud credits;
- donated technical and professional services;
- corporate social-impact sponsorship;
- educational and convening support;
- grants for open-source and standards work;
- fiscal sponsorship where appropriate and legally reviewed;
- donations or tax-deductible contributions **only after** the necessary legal and tax authority is authoritatively established.

The Foundation should maintain visible funding disclosures. Funding must not purchase a favorable risk decision, removal of a finding, a standards vote, certification language, suppression of minority views, or access to private participant data beyond authorized need.

### Funding models to avoid

The Foundation should not depend on sale of user data, sale of sensitive profiles, pay-to-remove findings, pay-to-pass reviews, undisclosed sponsor influence, manipulative engagement, clinical overclaiming, or certification fees before a separately governed assurance program exists.

## 8. Standards, governance, and ethical AI

The Foundation repository already contains a formation-stage governance architecture with a proposal lifecycle that moves through intake, triage, drafting, review, public comment, disposition, decision, publication, implementation, and reassessment.

Core governance principles include:

- independence from commercial pressure;
- conflicts of interest and recusals;
- affected-community participation;
- time-limited exceptions;
- appeals and reconsideration;
- preservation of minority opinions;
- narrow and temporary emergency authority;
- versioning, records, amendments, deprecations, and withdrawals;
- no invented reviewers, advisors, approvals, certifications, partnerships, or institutional claims.

The Ethical AI Principles cover human dignity, meaningful choice, informed consent, purpose limitation, minimization, private-by-default design, memory provenance, identity protection, third-party relationship rights, emotional safety, vulnerable populations, health-adjacent boundaries, accessibility, fairness, explainability, contestability, agentic authority, multi-model disagreement, provider accountability, security, spatial computing, synthetic media, grief/legacy, deletion/portability, rollback, human accountability, proportionality, environmental responsibility, and continuous review.

A defining principle is that the user remains the ultimate authority over their own life record. A model may assist, infer, reconstruct, or summarize, but generated material must not silently become unquestioned autobiographical truth.

## 9. Product accountability contract

The Foundation has created a Product Integration Contract that defines the evidence a user-facing or high-impact URAI system should maintain when claiming alignment.

The expected package includes:

1. system card and exact release identity;
2. provider register;
3. data-flow record;
4. consent map;
5. risk review;
6. accessibility record;
7. automation boundary;
8. transparency notice;
9. incident and exception log;
10. release, deployment, and rollback evidence.

The evidence-state model is deliberately conservative: `NOT ASSESSED`, `SELF-ATTESTED`, `REVIEW PENDING`, `REVIEWED WITH OPEN GAPS`, `TIME-LIMITED EXCEPTION`, `BLOCKED`, and `SUPERSEDED`.

The current model explicitly avoids `CERTIFIED`, `APPROVED`, or `COMPLIANT` until an independently governed and legally reviewed assurance program exists. The Foundation name must not become a rubber stamp.

## 10. Accessibility and inclusion

Accessibility is a cross-cutting standard rather than a side feature.

The Foundation's direction includes screen readers, keyboard and switch access, captions, transcripts, reduced motion, high contrast, plain language, predictable navigation, accessible authentication, user-controlled timeouts, low-stimulation modes, non-immersive alternatives, cognitive-load reduction, trauma-sensitive replay controls, and accessible complaint/feedback channels.

The ecosystem also contains dedicated thinking for Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, cognitive accessibility, veterans, older adults, caregivers, neurodivergent users, and people with sensory, motor, speech, memory, or cognitive access needs.

Accessibility claims must be evidence-based. A checklist is design guidance, not proof of conformance.

## 11. Claims, safety, and clinical boundary

URAI can be responsibly described as an AI-assisted memory, identity, reflection, accessibility, narrative-continuity, personal-world, and human-development platform.

The Foundation's claims-and-safety framework blocks unsupported assertions that URAI diagnoses conditions, treats trauma, treats PTSD, treats TBI, treats Alzheimer's or dementia, replaces therapy, predicts clinical outcomes, is VA-approved, or is clinically proven without studies.

Allowed direction is user-controlled and non-clinical: organizing memories, preserving life stories, reflecting on life patterns, exploring identity, visualizing goals, supporting caregiver/family connection, creating source-backed personal replays, tracking user-confirmed patterns, building narrative continuity, guided reflection, and improving accessibility of reflection and memory organization.

## 12. Research and evidence model

The Foundation is intended to create a responsible bridge between working technology and research.

A staged model is appropriate:

1. **Concept and safety framing** — define population, benefit, boundaries, risks, accessibility, consent, data handling, and non-clinical claims.
2. **Advisor and community review** — involve relevant scientific, accessibility, privacy, ethics, and affected-community expertise.
3. **Pilot design** — establish measurable goals, inclusion/exclusion criteria, user controls, escalation boundaries, retention, deletion, and safety rules without pre-claiming outcomes.
4. **Research determination** — if work becomes human-subject research, obtain the appropriate independent research/ethics review rather than relabeling product testing.
5. **Publication and correction** — publish only what evidence supports, disclose limitations, and preserve negative findings and corrections.

## 13. Public-interest program portfolio

The Foundation has documented program directions, but they should currently be described as **formation-stage concepts**, not active services.

### Memory Worlds for Seniors / Caregiver Connection Archive

A user-controlled approach to preserving memories, stories, routines, preferences, values, and identity continuity for older adults and families. It is intentionally non-medical and does not claim to diagnose, prevent, or treat dementia.

### Veteran Life Continuity Map

A voluntary, non-clinical life-story, transition, document, family-story, support-system, and future-planning experience. It must not imply VA approval, treatment of PTSD/TBI, benefits advice, or representation of all veterans.

### Cognitive Accessibility Interface

Low-stimulation, readable, predictable reflection and memory-organization patterns.

### User-Controlled Replay Design

Preview, skip, blur, lock, exit, correction, deletion, redaction, provenance, uncertainty, and explicit confirmation before generated content enters a durable personal record.

### Youth Identity and Future Worlds

Age-appropriate reflection/future-planning concepts that require separate minor-user governance, guardian boundaries, privacy restrictions, commercial limits, safety review, accessibility, and child-development/legal review before activation.

No concept should be marketed as an active program until the record identifies its authorized operator, governance approval, users, scope, location, duration, qualified reviewers, risk review, funding, consent/data handling, complaint path, measurable goals, launch date, and operating evidence.

## 16. Governance and independence model

The formation-stage charter identifies mature-institution roles such as Governance Steward, Standards Editor, Risk Review Chair, Transparency Editor, Accessibility Lead, Research Editor, Community Steward, Security Contact, Secretary/Records Steward, and qualified external legal/tax/research/accessibility reviewers.

Roles that are not formally appointed remain vacant. Repository ownership or technical maintenance does not, by itself, create an independent board or governance authority.

Long-term governance should include independent members, technical expertise, accessibility expertise, research/ethics competence, financial oversight, affected-community perspectives, conflict-management rules, records, term/succession processes, and clear authority.

The Foundation is designed around stewardship rather than permanent personal control. Institutional continuity should be carried by governing instruments, distributed expertise, records, succession/incapacity planning, financial separation, conflict rules, and documented handoff of credentials and duties.

## 17. Legal and financial boundary

The public narrative must distinguish operational substance from legal status.

The repository does not currently contain an authoritative, dated EIN receipt with a stable evidence locator. Any EIN referenced in restricted planning records therefore remains **unverified for public reliance** here and does not establish formation, good standing, fundraising authority, or federal tax-exempt status.

Current Texas formation/status evidence and operative governing-body/officer records still require authoritative verification before stronger legal, fundraising, tax-deductibility, or banking claims should be made.

Accordingly:

- Foundation identity, funds, accounting, cards, payees, and records must remain separate from URAI Labs LLC and other affiliates;
- the Foundation EIN must not be used as a substitute commercial-entity identity;
- source of funds must be described truthfully when funds actually exist;
- donations, grants, fundraising, fiscal sponsorship, banking, and restricted funds require the appropriate legal and governance authority;
- no public `501(c)(3)` or tax-deductible-giving claim should be made without authoritative recognition;
- governance roles and bank authority must come from adopted records, not repository labels.

This conservative boundary increases institutional credibility because the Foundation's own evidence rules apply to itself.

## 19. Impact model

The Foundation should measure impact without fabricating it.

A mature scorecard can include:

- **governance:** standards published, revisions completed, public comments dispositioned, exceptions closed, incidents reviewed, corrections published, independent reviewers engaged;
- **accessibility:** critical flows tested, barriers identified/remediated, affected-user reviews completed, caption/transcript coverage, non-immersive alternatives available;
- **user rights:** deletion completion, export success, correction turnaround, consent withdrawal, provenance visibility, contested-output resolution, autonomous-action reversal;
- **research/programs:** valid-authority pilots, protocol adherence, accessibility participation, partner diversity, publishable evidence, preservation of negative findings;
- **institutional health:** funding concentration, restricted/unrestricted funding mix, conflict disclosures, independent governance participation, current public disclosures, security-reporting-channel health, audit findings closed.

A public-interest institution should be willing to publish uncomfortable metrics.

## 20. Current status and executive partnership ask

Use a three-state model consistently:

### Operational / already built

A substantial URAI technical ecosystem exists. The Foundation repository, standards drafts, governance architecture, advisor materials, accessibility and claims policies, program concepts, grant pipeline, Drive diligence records, and publication tooling exist.

### Formation / authority hardening

Formal governing authority, current state-status evidence, board/officer records, fundraising authority, banking authority, and federal tax-exempt status require authoritative completion or verification before stronger claims are made.

### Future / activation-gated

Public programs, human-subject research, grant-funded pilots, public donations, formal certification, independent assurance, and impact claims activate only when their legal, governance, safety, funding, evidence, and operational gates are satisfied.

### Partnership ask

Help URAI Foundation turn an unusually deep working personal-AI ecosystem into a model for transparent, accessible, user-controlled, publicly accountable AI.

We are not asking partners to endorse every future claim. We are asking them to strengthen the infrastructure that makes claims reviewable.

We are not asking partners to take over governance. We are asking them to help independent governance become real.

We are not asking for permission to exploit private human data. The objective is to prove that the most valuable personal AI can also be the most accountable.

### One-sentence description

**URAI Foundation is a formation-stage public-interest initiative building governance, accessibility, research, ethics, and accountability frameworks around URAI so that increasingly powerful personal AI can remain answerable to the people whose lives it represents.**
