# Governance Charter

This charter describes how the URAI Foundation should make, review, and document decisions.

## Governance objectives

- Preserve independence from commercial pressure.
- Keep ethical standards public, reviewable, and traceable.
- Ensure decisions are informed by relevant expertise and affected communities.
- Maintain clear accountability for approvals, exceptions, and escalations.

## Decision categories

| Category | Examples | Minimum review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial | Typo fixes, formatting, clarifications | One maintainer |
| Policy | New or changed ethical standards | Two maintainers and subject-matter review |
| High-impact AI | Safety, privacy, rights, or public-interest implications | Formal risk review and documented approval |
| Emergency | Urgent safety, abuse, legal, or privacy concerns | Expedited maintainer action with post-incident review |

## Independence requirements

Foundation standards must not be overridden by commercial entities. Conflicts of interest should be disclosed in review discussions and decision records.

## Review expectations

Reviewers should evaluate proposals for:

- Alignment with the Ethical AI Principles.
- Evidence quality and clearly stated assumptions.
- Impact on affected users and communities.
- Privacy, safety, security, and fairness implications.
- Operational feasibility and accountability.

## Exceptions

Exceptions to published standards require documented rationale, scope, expiry date, owner, and mitigation plan. Open-ended exceptions should not be approved.

## Records

Significant decisions should be recorded in issues, pull requests, decision records, or release notes so future reviewers can understand what changed and why.
