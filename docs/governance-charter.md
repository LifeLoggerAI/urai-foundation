# Governance Charter

Status: formation-draft
Registry ID: `URAI-GOV-001`
Current governing body: not yet constituted

## Purpose and boundary

This charter defines the formation-stage operating rules for proposing, reviewing, publishing, amending, and retiring URAI Foundation standards and public-accountability materials.

It does not prove legal formation, nonprofit or tax-exempt status, a seated board, delegated regulatory authority, or an active certification program. Roles that are not formally appointed remain vacant.

## Governance objectives

- Preserve independence from commercial pressure.
- Keep standards public, versioned, reviewable, and traceable.
- Include relevant expertise and affected-community perspectives.
- Protect privacy, safety, accessibility, dignity, and user recourse.
- Identify authority, ownership, exceptions, and escalation paths.
- Separate Foundation standards from commercial product operation.
- Prevent unsupported claims about approval, certification, partnership, research, or impact.

## Appointment, terms, and vacancies

Before any person is publicly named in a governance role, the record should include:

- explicit acceptance and permission to publish;
- scope and authority;
- start date and term or review date;
- relevant qualifications or community perspective;
- conflict disclosure;
- removal/resignation process;
- access granted;
- confidentiality obligations where appropriate.

Vacancies must be shown as vacant. No fictional interim title, board seat, advisor, reviewer, or community representative may be created to satisfy a process requirement.

Future formal governance should define term lengths, staggered terms, limits, removal for cause, resignation, incapacity, and succession.

## Decision categories

| Category | Examples | Minimum formation-stage review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial | Typo, formatting, non-substantive clarity | One maintainer; checks pass |
| Website/operations | Metadata, navigation, CI, publication build | Maintainer plus affected technical reviewer |
| Process | Templates, contribution flow, evidence rules | Two reviewers or one reviewer plus public review |
| Standards | New or materially changed requirements | Standards Editor, relevant subject-matter review, decision record, changelog/version update |
| High-impact AI | Safety, rights, privacy, vulnerable users, autonomy, memory/identity, spatial systems | Formal risk review, relevant expert and affected-community perspectives, named approval role |
| Legal/organizational | Entity, tax, fundraising, contracts, licensing, research participation | Qualified legal/tax review and explicit owner authorization |
| Emergency | Active safety, security, privacy, abuse, or integrity incident | Narrow temporary action, documented owner, expiry, and retrospective review |

## Proposal lifecycle

1. **Intake** — assign an identifier, owner role, scope, status, and evidence links.
2. **Triage** — classify the change and identify required reviewers, conflicts, and legal sensitivity.
3. **Draft** — state problem, affected populations, requirements, non-goals, evidence, risks, compatibility, and open questions.
4. **Review** — perform technical, privacy, security, accessibility, community, research, and legal review as applicable.
5. **Public comment** — open a defined period for material standards unless urgency or confidentiality justifies a documented exception.
6. **Disposition** — record how comments were accepted, rejected, deferred, or found out of scope.
7. **Decision** — approve, approve with conditions, return for revision, reject, withdraw, or defer.
8. **Publication** — update registry, version, changelog, canonical files, and effective/transition information.
9. **Implementation** — affected product owners document applicability, gaps, exceptions, and release evidence.
10. **Reassessment** — review after incidents, legal changes, material product changes, expiry, or credible external concern.

## Quorum and voting during formation

No multi-person quorum currently exists unless appointments are separately documented.

Until a governing group is constituted:

- editorial and technical repository maintenance may be approved by the repository owner after checks;
- material standards changes should remain `formation-draft` unless at least two qualified reviewers cover the relevant domains;
- a single commercially interested person must not label a standard independently adopted;
- legal, research, accessibility-conformance, certification, partnership, grant, donation, and institutional claims require external evidence and review;
- absence of reviewers is a blocker, not permission to invent approval.

A future constituted body should define:

- quorum as a majority of filled voting seats, with at least three eligible voters for material standards decisions;
- simple majority for ordinary decisions;
- two-thirds for new adopted standards, breaking changes, emergency extensions, governance amendments, and removal for cause;
- unanimous approval is not required unless a future legal instrument says otherwise;
- abstentions do not count as affirmative votes;
- recused members do not count toward the eligible-voter denominator for that matter, but a minimum of two non-conflicted reviewers should remain for material decisions.

These thresholds are a proposed formation model and require approval before operational use.

## Conflicts of interest and recusals

Reviewers should disclose financial, employment, ownership, funding, advisory, close personal, product-responsibility, partner, and litigation interests that could reasonably affect judgment.

A conflicted person may provide facts and answer questions but should not control the decision. The record should state:

- the disclosed interest;
- whether recusal is required;
- what participation remains allowed;
- the replacement reviewer or reason no replacement is available;
- any mitigation;
- the final decision-maker.

Commercial product ownership creates a standing interest in product-specific standards decisions. It does not automatically disqualify factual participation, but independent or affected-community review should be added before claiming external credibility.

## Commercial influence protection

No commercial entity may silently override, delete, suppress, or misrepresent Foundation standards or findings.

Commercial funding, sponsorship, donated services, provider credits, or product support should be disclosed. Funding does not purchase:

- a favorable risk decision;
- removal of a finding;
- a standards vote;
- certification or approval language;
- exclusive control over public comment;
- suppression of minority views;
- access to private participant or reporter data beyond authorized need.

A product may choose not to implement Foundation guidance, but the resulting gap or exception should remain visible when alignment is claimed.

## Affected-community participation

Material standards should identify affected populations and seek relevant perspectives without implying that one participant represents an entire community.

Participation should be:

- voluntary and informed;
- accessible;
- compensated when sustained expert labor is requested and funding permits;
- non-extractive;
- privacy-preserving;
- clear about influence and limits;
- documented without exposing unnecessary personal information.

## Public comment

Material standards should normally have a public-comment period with:

- proposal/version;
- opening and closing dates;
- scope and questions;
- safe participation channels;
- accessibility accommodations;
- moderation rules;
- comment dispositions and rationale;
- final decision link.

Public GitHub issues are not appropriate for sensitive reports. If no verified private channel exists, sensitive intake remains blocked.

## Exceptions

An exception must include:

- requirement being waived;
- scope and affected systems/users;
- evidence and rationale;
- risk and mitigation;
- owner;
- approval role;
- start and expiry date;
- monitoring and user notice where appropriate;
- rollback/disablement trigger;
- public summary or reason for confidentiality.

Open-ended exceptions are invalid. Renewal requires new evidence and review.

## Appeals and reconsideration

A person or product owner may request reconsideration when:

- material evidence was missed;
- process was not followed;
- a conflict was undisclosed;
- the decision creates a serious unintended impact;
- new facts or law materially change the analysis.

The appeal should be reviewed by a person who did not control the original decision when feasible. The record should state scope, evidence, reviewer conflicts, result, and whether the original decision remains in force during review.

## Minority opinions

A qualified reviewer may publish a concise minority opinion when disagreement remains material. It should state evidence, reasoning, risks, and proposed alternative without personal attack. Minority opinions should remain linked to the final decision and may trigger future reassessment.

## Emergency authority

Emergency action must be necessary, narrow, time-limited, and documented. It may temporarily disable a feature, withhold unsafe details, revoke a publication, restrict access, or correct a dangerous claim.

Emergency action must not be used to bypass ordinary review for convenience. Within a defined review window, the record should include:

- triggering event;
- authority used;
- scope;
- people/systems affected;
- duration;
- evidence;
- follow-up decision;
- corrective actions;
- public summary when safe.

## Records and retention

Material records should include:

- proposals and versions;
- agendas and minutes;
- reviewer roles;
- conflict disclosures and recusals;
- comments and dispositions;
- decisions and minority opinions;
- risk reviews, exceptions, incidents, corrections, and retractions;
- releases, deployed/rollback SHAs, and implementation reports.

A future retention schedule requires legal and privacy review. Sensitive records should not be placed in a public repository merely to satisfy transparency.

## Standards amendment, deprecation, and withdrawal

Breaking changes should include rationale, affected parties, transition guidance, effective date, and owner. Superseded standards remain archived and linked to replacements.

Withdrawal or retraction should preserve a safe public notice explaining status and reason. Material history should not be silently erased.

## Transition to formal governance

Before this charter is presented as legally operative governance, qualified counsel and the authorized governing body should review:

- legal entity and jurisdiction;
- board/steward authority and fiduciary duties;
- bylaws and committee structure;
- IP ownership/license from URAI IP Holdings LLC;
- privacy, records, employment/contractor, insurance, finance, and tax obligations;
- research and human-subject protections;
- fundraising, donations, grants, and fiscal sponsorship;
- standards liability and assurance/certification boundaries.

## Acceptance rule

A material decision is procedurally complete only when:

- authority and reviewer roles are identified;
- conflicts are disclosed and handled;
- required evidence and perspectives are present;
- comments are dispositioned or an exception is documented;
- decision, conditions, expiry, and appeal path are recorded;
- registry/version/changelog are updated when applicable;
- no unsupported legal, institutional, certification, research, partner, program, or impact claim is introduced.
