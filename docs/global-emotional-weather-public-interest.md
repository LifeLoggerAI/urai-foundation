# Global Emotional Weather and Humanitarian Attention

Status: formation-draft
Evidence class: E1/E2 depending on linked exact-SHA implementation evidence
Program status: not an active Foundation research program

## Purpose

This document defines the public-interest governance boundary for URAI's proposed Global Emotional Weather and humanitarian-attention capability.

The capability is intended to help identify meaningful population-level changes using voluntary, privacy-protected aggregate signals and corroborating public evidence.

It is not intended to expose individual people or to convert emotional inference into an emergency declaration.

## Current authority boundary

This formation draft does not establish:

- an active human-subject research study;
- participant recruitment;
- IRB or ethics-board approval;
- an academic, NGO, government, UN, or humanitarian partnership;
- a validated famine, conflict, disease, or disaster prediction system;
- clinical or diagnostic authority;
- emergency-management authority;
- Foundation certification of the commercial URAI product.

Any future claim in those categories requires separate authoritative evidence and review.

## Permitted public-interest framing

A properly governed implementation may:

- observe privacy-protected regional Emotional Weather aggregates;
- identify statistically or operationally meaningful changes;
- join those observations to authoritative external public-interest evidence;
- surface a region for human investigation or review;
- publish uncertainty, provenance, freshness, method version, and limitations;
- suppress output when privacy or evidence requirements are not met.

It must distinguish:

1. observation;
2. change detected;
3. corroborated signal;
4. humanitarian watch;
5. humanitarian attention;
6. insufficient evidence.

## Prohibited interpretation

The system must not represent:

- emotion as proof of famine, conflict, disease, or disaster;
- correlation as causation;
- a heuristic as validated prediction;
- a model output as an emergency declaration;
- lack of data as safety;
- a regional aggregate as a statement about every person in that region.

## Individual privacy boundary

The population system must not provide a route from a public region to an individual.

Public-interest outputs must exclude:

- names;
- user IDs;
- pseudonyms;
- exact addresses;
- exact coordinates;
- individual movement trails;
- memories;
- transcripts;
- raw audio/video/images;
- relationship records;
- raw health records;
- individual emotional histories.

Pseudonymization alone is not anonymization.

## Consent boundary

Participation must be separate, explicit, voluntary, revocable, and purpose-specific.

Public-good aggregate research participation must not be bundled with:

- personalization;
- passive context;
- location permission;
- sensitive inference permission;
- biometric permission;
- monetization.

Revocation must stop future contribution and remove eligible unreleased contributions where technically possible.

Already released genuinely anonymous aggregates may not always be mathematically reversible to remove one person's contribution; this limitation must be stated accurately.

## Release requirements

A regional release should require:

- active purpose-specific consent upstream;
- a sufficiently large cohort;
- suppression of sparse or low-confidence cells;
- governed geography;
- repeated-query protection;
- bounded privacy budget;
- exact release/version provenance;
- timestamp/freshness;
- uncertainty;
- no individual drill-down;
- kill switch;
- auditability.

Higher cohort thresholds may be required for sensitive location/emotional patterns.

## Humanitarian evidence requirements

Humanitarian attention must use corroborating external evidence where appropriate.

Potential evidence categories include:

- weather and drought;
- crop conditions;
- food availability and prices;
- disaster events;
- displacement;
- public-health indicators;
- infrastructure disruption;
- connectivity or electricity disruption.

Every source should retain:

- source/provider;
- source timestamp;
- request timestamp;
- geographic resolution;
- license/usage authority;
- units/normalization;
- limitations;
- confidence;
- provenance locator.

No source should be treated as authoritative outside its actual scope.

## Human review

Consequential humanitarian interpretation should require human review unless a narrower authority is separately approved.

A review record should expose:

- trigger reason;
- observed change;
- supporting evidence;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- data freshness;
- method version;
- known limitations;
- reviewer decision;
- corrections;
- audit history.

## High-risk review classification

Population-scale location/emotional analysis should be treated as high-risk under the Foundation risk-review process because it combines sensitive inference, geography, large-scale public impact, and potential vulnerable populations.

Deployment should remain blocked unless privacy, security, accessibility, evidence, rollback, monitoring, and qualified review requirements are satisfied.

## Accessibility

A public map must not communicate meaning by color alone.

It should support:

- keyboard navigation;
- screen-reader summaries;
- text equivalents;
- high contrast;
- reduced motion;
- reduced stimulation;
- touch targets;
- mobile use;
- accessible non-map summaries.

## Research boundary

Before using this system for human-subject research, qualified reviewers must determine:

- whether the activity is research or product evaluation;
- required consent and withdrawal;
- vulnerable-population protections;
- ethics/institutional review;
- re-identification risk;
- retention and sharing rules;
- publication risk;
- compensation/non-coercion where applicable.

The Foundation must not self-declare formal review unnecessary for high-risk studies.

## Evidence and publication

Claims should follow the Foundation evidence grades.

Source code and exact-SHA automated tests may support E2 engineering evidence.

They do not by themselves prove:

- real-world humanitarian effectiveness;
- population-scale validity;
- fairness across countries/languages;
- causal prediction;
- independent review;
- production deployment;
- public benefit.

Those claims require stronger external evidence.

## Kill-switch principle

Population contribution, aggregate publication, and humanitarian-attention publication should be independently disableable.

Disabling the public-good population layer must not disable a person's private URAI experience.

## Acceptance boundary

This formation draft may be used to review implementation alignment.

It is not an approval, certification, partnership, program launch, or humanitarian authority record.
