# Transparency Framework

This framework sets expectations for public documentation and accountability.

## Required disclosures

Public-facing AI systems should disclose:

- System purpose and intended users.
- Known limitations and inappropriate uses.
- Whether users are interacting with AI-generated content or decisions.
- Data categories used for training, evaluation, or operation when disclosure is possible and safe.
- Material human oversight and appeal mechanisms.
- Contact paths for concerns, errors, and harm reports.

## Documentation levels

| Level | Use when | Expected artifacts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | Low-risk documentation or internal tooling | README, owner, change history |
| Standard | User-facing AI functionality | Model/system card, risk review, user guidance |
| Enhanced | High-impact, sensitive, or public-interest systems | Independent review, evaluation summaries, incident process, periodic reassessment |

## Incident transparency

When a material safety, privacy, or rights-impacting incident occurs, maintainers should document:

1. What happened.
2. Who may be affected.
3. Immediate mitigations.
4. Longer-term corrective actions.
5. How similar incidents will be prevented or detected.

## Limits on disclosure

Transparency should not expose secrets, personal data, active abuse vectors, or information that materially increases risk. When details are withheld, the reason should be stated at an appropriate level.
