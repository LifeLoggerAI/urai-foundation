Community listening

Public-interest technology should begin by listening.

URAI Foundation’s community work is framed around access, practical education, participation, responsible technology, and people who are often left out of technology design.

Start a community conversationVerify current status

Current boundary: Programs, events, grants, partnerships, volunteer roles, and services are described as active only when separately verified. This page does not create eligibility, representation, a promise of assistance, or a fundraising solicitation.

Community standard

Meet people with dignity, make information understandable, design for access, and leave room for communities to shape the technology that affects them.

Participation framework

A public front door without pretending a program is already live.

Community learning

Plain-language sessions, demonstrations, digital-literacy support, and responsible-AI education when programs are established and published.

Accessibility outreach

Work shaped around visual, haptic, cognitive, communication, mobility, and sensory access, with community review rather than assumed representation.

Local collaboration

Schools, nonprofits, community organizations, researchers, and public-interest groups can explore ideas without implying a partnership before it is confirmed.

Volunteer interest

People may express interest in outreach, events, accessibility review, research support, storytelling, or community operations; no role is implied until published.

Community listening

Feedback, lived experience, concerns, and ideas can inform priorities and public standards without turning participation into data extraction.

Public accountability

Any future program should publish what it is, who it serves, what was learned, what changed, and what remains uncertain.
